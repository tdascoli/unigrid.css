Welcome

Yellowstone was established as the world's first national park in 1872. The park spans 2.2 million acres of breathtaking wilderness, featuring the most extraordinary collection of geothermal features on Earth.

The park sits atop one of the world's largest active volcanic systems, with over 10,000 thermal features — including more than 500 geysers, representing about half of all geysers on Earth.

Did You Know?

Yellowstone's Grand Prismatic Spring is the largest hot spring in the United States and the third largest in the world, measuring approximately 370 feet in diameter.

Natural Features

Yellowstone's geothermal features are the result of an active volcanic hotspot beneath the park. The Yellowstone Caldera, formed during the last catastrophic eruption 640,000 years ago, measures about 34 by 45 miles.

The park contains the majority of the world's active geysers — more than any other place on Earth. Old Faithful erupts approximately every 90 minutes, shooting 3,700 to 8,400 gallons of boiling water to heights of 106 to 185 feet.

The Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone is roughly 20 miles long, up to 1,200 feet deep, and between 1,500 and 4,000 feet wide. The canyon features two major waterfalls: the Upper Falls at 109 feet and the Lower Falls at 308 feet.

Accessibility

Many boardwalks at thermal areas are wheelchair accessible. Accessible lodging and campgrounds available. Service animals welcome.

Safety

Stay on boardwalks in thermal areas. Water in hot springs can cause severe burns. Ground may be fragile and thin. Swimming in thermal features is prohibited.

Bear Safety

Carry bear spray and know how to use it. Make noise on trails. Never approach wildlife. Store food in bear-proof containers. Report all bear encounters to a ranger.
