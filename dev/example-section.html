Grand Teton National Park

Rising Above the Valley

The Teton Range rises abruptly more than 7,000 feet above the valley floor of Jackson Hole — a dramatic landscape shaped by ancient glaciers.

Overview

Grand Teton National Park preserves a spectacular landscape rich with majestic mountains, pristine lakes, and extraordinary wildlife. The park has more than 200 miles of trails to explore, from easy lakeside walks to strenuous mountain ascents.

Established in 1929, the park encompasses 310,000 acres in northwestern Wyoming. The Teton Range, the youngest range in the Rocky Mountains, includes the 13,775-foot Grand Teton — the highest peak in the park.

Quick Facts

Established: 1929
Area: 310,000 acres
Highest point: 13,775 ft
Annual visitors: 3.5 million

Things To Do

Hiking, climbing, fishing, and more

Hiking

Over 200 miles of trails range from easy lakeside walks to challenging alpine routes. Popular trails include Cascade Canyon, Lake Solitude, and the Paintbrush-Cascade loop.

Mountaineering

The Teton Range offers world-class climbing. The Grand Teton itself attracts thousands of climbers each year. Registration is required for all overnight climbs.

Wildlife Viewing

Home to moose, elk, bison, black bears, grizzly bears, and wolves. Dawn and dusk offer the best viewing opportunities, especially along the Moose-Wilson Road.

Fishing

Snake River and the park's many lakes offer excellent fishing for cutthroat, lake, and brown trout. A Wyoming fishing license is required.

Boating

Motorized and non-motorized boats are permitted on Jackson, Jenny, and Phelps lakes. Permits required. Kayaking and canoeing are popular on String and Leigh lakes.

Winter Sports

Cross-country skiing, snowshoeing, and backcountry skiing are available in winter. The Teton Park Road is groomed for skiing from mid-December through mid-March.

Plan Your Visit

Entrance Fees

Vehicle: $35 (7 days)
Individual: $20
Annual Pass: $70

Seasons

Summer: May–Oct
Winter: Dec–Mar
Peak: July–August

Getting Here

Jackson Hole Airport (JAC) is inside the park. Also accessible via Yellowstone's South Entrance.

Lodging

5 campgrounds, several lodges. Reservations strongly recommended for summer months.

Safety

Bear Safety

Carry bear spray and know how to use it. Make noise on trails. Store all food in bear-proof containers. Never approach wildlife.

Mountain Weather

Weather changes rapidly in the Tetons. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in summer. Bring layers, rain gear, and check forecasts before heading out.

Contact & Information

Craig Thomas Discovery & Visitor Center

Located in Moose, WY. Open year-round. Exhibits, bookstore, and ranger programs. Park information and permits available.

Jenny Lake Visitor Center

Seasonal visitor center with geology exhibits and ranger talks. Open mid-May through late September. Starting point for popular hikes.
